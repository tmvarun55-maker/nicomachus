# Foundations: what the field is actually built on

## Judgement and decision making

**Heuristics and biases** (Kahneman & Tversky, from 1974). Judgement under
uncertainty runs on shortcuts that are efficient and systematically biased.
The durable ones:

- *Anchoring* — numeric estimates pull toward an arbitrary presented value.
  One of the most robustly replicated effects in the field, surviving the
  replication crisis intact.
- *Availability* — frequency judged by ease of retrieval. Explains most
  systematic public misestimation of risk.
- *Representativeness* — probability judged by resemblance to a prototype;
  produces base-rate neglect and the conjunction fallacy (the Linda problem).
- *Framing / prospect theory* (1979) — outcomes evaluated as gains or losses
  from a reference point, with a concave value function for gains, convex for
  losses, and steeper for losses than gains. The core is well supported; the
  exact 2:1 loss-aversion coefficient is contested and appears smaller and
  more contextual than the canonical figure. Gal & Rucker's critique is worth
  reading.
- *Overconfidence* — calibration failures, particularly in interval estimates
  and in professional judgement.
- *Confirmation bias* — the tendency to seek and weight confirming evidence.
  Wason's selection task is the classic demonstration.

**Dual process theory** (Stanovich & West's System 1 / System 2, popularised
in *Thinking, Fast and Slow*). Useful as an organising metaphor; taken
literally as two systems it is contested (Keren & Schul, Melnikoff & Bargh,
Osman's single-process alternatives). Kahneman himself acknowledged in 2017
that the priming chapter of his own book rested on evidence that has not held.

**Gigerenzer's counter-position** deserves equal time: heuristics are not
defective reasoning but *ecologically rational* — fast and frugal rules that
outperform complex models in environments with the right structure. The
"biases" often disappear when probabilities are presented as natural
frequencies rather than percentages, a finding with real applied value in
medical risk communication.

## Personality

**The Five-Factor Model / Big Five**: Openness, Conscientiousness,
Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism. Derived lexically and factor-
analytically, replicated across many languages and cultures. Predictive
validity is real but modest — conscientiousness predicts job performance
(ρ ≈ .2) and longevity; neuroticism predicts psychopathology. Note the
recent challenge: large studies in non-WEIRD and low-literacy samples
(Gurven et al. on the Tsimane) do not always recover the five-factor
structure, suggesting it may be partly a product of educated, literate
populations.

**HEXACO** (Ashton & Lee) adds Honesty–Humility as a sixth factor and captures
variance the Big Five misses, particularly for the "dark" traits.

**Dark Triad / Tetrad** — narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy (plus
everyday sadism). Studied as individual differences; measured by the SD3 and
Dirty Dozen. Worth knowing that these instruments were designed for research
on *variation in a population*, not for diagnosing an individual, and that
casual application of the labels to people in one's life is neither what they
measure nor what they support.

**Not supported**: MBTI (poor test–retest reliability, dichotomised
continuous traits, no predictive validity for job performance), Enneagram,
DISC as psychometrics, learning styles (a well-tested and well-falsified
claim — matching instruction to a purported style does not improve learning).

## Motivation

**Self-Determination Theory** (Deci & Ryan) — three basic psychological
needs: autonomy, competence, relatedness. Well-supported, cross-culturally
replicated, and the most useful applied framework in the area. Its major
claim, that extrinsic rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation for already-
interesting tasks, is supported by meta-analysis (Deci, Koestner & Ryan, 1999)
with boundary conditions.

Note the connection to manipulation: SDT gives a principled reason why
autonomy-supportive influence produces durable change and controlling
influence produces compliance that decays. See [manipulation-and-defence].

**Goal-setting theory** (Locke & Latham) — specific, difficult goals with
feedback outperform "do your best." Robust, with a large applied literature.

**Growth mindset** (Dweck) — the intervention effects have shrunk
substantially under replication and meta-analysis (Sisk et al., 2018; Macnamara
& Burgoyne, 2023). Large pre-registered trials find effects near zero for most
students, with possible small benefits for disadvantaged or low-achieving
students. Teach it with these numbers attached.

## Memory

- **Reconstructive, not reproductive** (Bartlett, 1932). Memory is rebuilt at
  retrieval from schema plus fragments.
- **Misinformation effect and false memory** (Elizabeth Loftus). Post-event
  information alters testimony; whole false autobiographical events can be
  implanted in a substantial minority of participants. Consequential: this
  work reshaped eyewitness identification procedure and is central to the
  recovered-memory disputes.
- **Testing effect and spacing effect** — retrieval practice and distributed
  study beat re-reading, robustly and by large margins. Among the best-
  supported applied findings in all of psychology, and the most ignored.
- **Working memory** (Baddeley & Hitch) — phonological loop, visuospatial
  sketchpad, central executive, episodic buffer.

## Social

- **Social identity theory** (Tajfel & Turner) — minimal group paradigm shows
  in-group favouritism arises from mere categorisation. Robust.
- **Fundamental attribution error / correspondence bias** — over-attributing
  others' behaviour to disposition and one's own to situation. Real, but
  culturally variable (weaker in East Asian samples; Choi, Nisbett & Norenzayan).
- **Bystander effect** — real, but a 2019 meta-analysis of CCTV footage of
  real public conflicts (Philpot et al.) found intervention in about 90% of
  cases, and that *more* bystanders raised the chance someone intervened. The
  lab effect and the field reality point in different directions.
- **Conformity** (Asch) — replicates, with roughly a third of critical-trial
  responses conforming, and substantial cultural and era variation
  (Bond & Smith's meta-analysis).
- **Contact hypothesis** (Allport) — intergroup contact reduces prejudice
  under conditions (equal status, common goals, cooperation, institutional
  support). Pettigrew & Tropp's meta-analysis of 500+ studies supports it,
  with publication-bias caveats.

## Development and attachment

**Attachment theory** (Bowlby; Ainsworth's Strange Situation) — secure,
anxious-ambivalent, avoidant, and later disorganised (Main & Solomon)
patterns. Well-evidenced in infancy. The adult attachment literature is more
contested: the mapping from infant classification to adult romantic style is
weaker than popular accounts suggest, and longitudinal continuity is modest.
The popular "attachment styles" discourse considerably overstates it.

**Piaget's stages** — the sequence is broadly supported, the sharp stage
boundaries and the ages are not; infants demonstrate competencies far earlier
than his tasks detected (Baillargeon's violation-of-expectation work).

**Vygotsky** — zone of proximal development and the social origin of higher
cognition. Less falsifiable, more influential in education.

## The standing caveat

Read [research-methods] before treating any of the above as settled. Several
entries in a standard textbook would not survive a pre-registered replication,
and the honest position on many of them is "real phenomenon, smaller and more
conditional than reported."
