# Social psychology

The field hit hardest by the replication crisis, and therefore the one where
knowing the current status of each claim matters most. Read
[research-methods] first.

## Groups and identity

**Social identity theory** (Tajfel & Turner). The **minimal group paradigm** —
assign people to groups by coin flip, and they still allocate more to their
own group. Robust and widely replicated. Self-esteem is partly derived from
group membership, which is why identity threat produces defensive behaviour.

**Self-categorisation theory** (Turner) extends it: the self is construed at
different levels (personal, group, human), and which level is salient shifts
behaviour. This underlies **Haslam & Reicher's** reinterpretation of the
obedience studies as *engaged followership* — identification with a cause
rather than submission to authority. See [persuasion-science] for the Milgram
and Zimbardo caveats, which are substantial.

**Realistic conflict theory** (Sherif's Robbers Cave) — competition over
scarce resources generates hostility; superordinate goals reduce it. Note
that Sherif ran three such camps and reported the one that worked; the others
failed, and the selective reporting was not disclosed. Gina Perry's archival
work documents this. Teach the finding with that attached.

## Prejudice and its reduction

**Contact hypothesis** (Allport). Intergroup contact reduces prejudice under
conditions: equal status, common goals, cooperation, institutional support.
Pettigrew & Tropp's meta-analysis (515 studies) supports it, with the
conditions being facilitating rather than strictly necessary. Caveats:
publication bias, and most studies are cross-sectional, so selection (less
prejudiced people seek contact) is hard to rule out.

**Paluck, Green & Green (2021)** reviewed the prejudice-reduction field and
found the evidence base far weaker than its influence: few field experiments,
short follow-ups, heavy reliance on student samples and self-report.

**Implicit bias and the IAT.** The IAT reliably measures *something* and
shows population-level effects. But: test–retest reliability is poor (r ≈ .5,
too low for individual assessment), the correlation with actual discriminatory
behaviour is small (meta-analytic r ≈ .15 or lower), and changes in IAT scores
do not produce changes in behaviour (Forscher et al., 2019). **Implicit bias
training** has little evidence of durable behavioural effect. This is one of
the largest gaps between commercial adoption and evidence in the field, and
saying so is not a political position — it is what the meta-analyses report.

**Deep canvassing** (Broockman & Kalla, 2016) remains the best-evidenced
intervention: non-judgemental, perspective-taking conversation producing
durable attitude change measured months later, pre-registered and replicated.
Notably it works by eliciting the other person's own reflection — the
self-persuasion mechanism in [persuasion-science].

## Norms

**Descriptive** (what people do) versus **injunctive** (what people approve
of) norms — Cialdini, Reno & Kallgren. Confusing them backfires: telling
people that many others litter *increases* littering even while disapproving
of it. Schultz's **boomerang effect** is the same phenomenon in energy use.

Norm interventions are the best-supported behaviour-change lever available at
scale, and the effects are small — 1–2% in the large field trials. Real,
replicated, and routinely overstated by an order of magnitude in popular
accounts.

**Pluralistic ignorance** — privately rejecting a norm while believing others
accept it, so everyone publicly conforms. Well-documented, and the mechanism
behind several successful "correcting misperceptions" interventions.

## Helping and harming

**Bystander effect.** The lab effect is real and replicates. But Philpot et
al. (2019) analysed CCTV of 219 real public conflicts across three countries
and found intervention in **90%** of cases, with *more* bystanders raising the
probability that someone intervened. The lab and the street point opposite
ways. The honest teaching is that diffusion of responsibility is a real
psychological process whose net field effect is swamped by others.

**Aggression.** Frustration–aggression, social learning (Bandura's Bobo doll —
which shows imitation of a novel behaviour, not that media causes violence).
The violent-video-games literature is genuinely contested, with
meta-analyses disagreeing and substantial publication bias evidence.

## Attitudes and behaviour

The **attitude–behaviour gap** is the field's oldest embarrassment (LaPiere,
1934). **Theory of planned behaviour** (Ajzen) — attitude, subjective norm,
perceived control → intention → behaviour — is the standard model, criticised
for being near-unfalsifiable and for the intention–behaviour gap it inherits.
**Implementation intentions** (Gollwitzer) — specifying when, where and how —
is one of the more robust practical findings for closing that gap.

## What to hold loosely

Not replicated, or much weaker than reported: ego depletion, most social
priming, facial feedback (contested), power posing's physiological claims,
Stanford Prison as evidence for anything, stereotype threat's larger effects
(shrinks substantially with bias correction), growth-mindset interventions at
scale, implicit bias training.

Robust: minimal group effects, anchoring, conformity (with cultural
variation), descriptive norms, contact effects, implementation intentions,
the mere-exposure effect, cognitive dissonance in its induced-compliance form.
